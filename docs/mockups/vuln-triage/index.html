The triage model

Every open finding is in exactly one bucket, decided by the first rule that matches, in this order. The bucket never changes a finding's state, its severity, or the open count. Missing or stale data makes a rule not fire, so a finding can only stay in a more cautious bucket.

Review questions for the owner

The full list with recommended defaults is in the epic spec, "Open questions". The ones a screen decides:

  1. V1 (epic Q1). Bucket names: "Fix available", "Needs attention", "Low risk", "Ignorable". Recommended. Alternative: "Safe to ignore" for the last. Every screen.
  2. V2 (Q2). Debian end-of-life is Needs attention, not ignorable. Recommended. host-vulns.html
  3. V8 (Q8). Old kernels not running and kernel headers are Ignorable, with "remove old kernels" as a tidy-up. Recommended. host-vulns.html, risk-overview.html
  4. V9 (Q9). "In use: N processes" and "Listening" need ptrace read access on the agent. Recommended: off unless the VM enables it; then the signals appear only on those hosts. states.html
  5. V10 (Q10, Q11). Decisions: reason and expiry required (90 days, 30 for KEV), no second factor except for more than 10 hosts at once. Recommended. override.html
  6. V12 (Q12). The exposure score is a published points table with a tier word. Recommended. Alternative: tier only. tiles.html
  7. V14 (Q14). Host tab section order: Fix available first (recommended) or Needs attention first. host-vulns.html
  8. V15 (Q15). The fleet list's "Open vulnerabilities" column becomes Exposure and Work. Recommended. fleet.html
  9. V16 (Q16). A new top-level Risk page after Compliance. Recommended. Alternative: these panels on Compliance. risk-overview.html

Wording, data, and accessibility

  • Hosts are *.example.lan. Advisory and CVE ids such as DEBIAN-CVE-2026-90777 are invented and name no real vulnerability; the KEV listing and EPSS scores shown are invented too. Package names are real Debian names with invented versions.
  • No badge says "secure", "safe", "clean", "compliant", or "protected"; every badge states its claim and scope. Nothing ranks people.
  • No JavaScript, no inline styles, and no external requests: the screens render offline and under the app's CSP (style-src 'self'). Bars and charts are SVG with geometry in attributes and colours in triage.css.
  • Buckets, tiers, signals, and badges are words plus shapes, never colour alone; meters are native <meter> with the numbers in text; charts have a title, a description, and a table of the numbers; sections are native <details>; forms have labels and errors beside the field.
